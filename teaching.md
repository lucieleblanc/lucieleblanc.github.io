# Teaching

I'm interested in how people learn. I admire engineering team cultures that promote teaching and learning.

I started working as a teaching assistant during my second year at Columbia.

## Course Assistant

For the following classes, I served as a course assistant. My responsibilities included holding office hours, managing student Q&A via a Listserv or dedicated forum, running review sessions, grading assignments, and grading exams.

### [COMS 3157](https://cs3157.github.io/) - Advanced Programming
_Spring 2019, Fall 2019*, Spring 2020*, Fall 2020, Spring 2021_

Columbia's systems programming [course with a narrative](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~jae/papers/3157-paper-v2.2-camera-final.pdf), taught by [Jae Woo Lee](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~jae/): C programming, pointers, UNIX command line and shell environment, TCP/IP, and client-server architecture. This is one of the required courses for the CS major, with enrollements of around 300 students and a teaching staff of 15. Before Spring 2020, the teaching staff also organized a class hackathon each term.

I served as Head TA in semesters marked with an asterisk (*):
* Onboarded new TAs
* Supervised and gave feedback on other TAs' review sessions and grading work
* Ran weekly teaching staff meetings
* Administered the course Linux server used by students and teaching staff to write, submit and grade assignments
* Refining lesson plans for weekly review sessions

## Pedagogical approach

My pedagogical approach centers around finding consistent, shared spoken and visual language to express new concepts, then developing fluency in that language the same way we develop fluency in world languages. It's especially important for instructors to spend time developing, refining, and practicing their instructional language.

I'm interested in working on the usability of programming languages, tools and frameworks to make them more intuitive, usable and understandable to a diverse audience.
